How does a robot drive straight?

        
Read from top to bottom. Everything here moves. Try the sliders and buttons!

      


      

        

          1
          
Meet the robot

          
This is a SPIKE Prime driving base.

          
    
            
  • The hub is the robot's brain. It runs your code.
    • 
            
  • The gyro is hidden inside the hub. It feels when the robot turns.
    • 
            
  • Two motors spin the two wheels.
    • 
            
  • A metal ball in the back lets it glide.
    • 
          

          
👆 Drag the robot to spin it around.

        

        

      


      

        

          2
          
Why does it drift?

          
We give both motors the same power. But no two motors are exactly the same!

          
If one wheel turns a little slower, the robot slowly curves toward that side. That is called drift.

          

            
            
          

        

        

      


      

        

          3
          
F is the go power

          
F tells the motors how hard to push. The see-through ghost drives at the perfect speed.

          
Too little F and your robot falls behind. Too much and it zooms ahead. Find the F that keeps up!

          

            
            
            
…

          

        

        

      


      

        

          4
          
The gyro feels turns

          
The gyro is like the dizzy feeling you get when you spin around. It tells the robot how much it has turned.

          
    
            
  • Turned right → a plus number, like +20.
    • 
            
  • Turned left → a minus number, like −20.
    • 
            
  • Pointing straight → 0.
    • 
          

          
👆 Drag the robot to turn it.

        

        

      


      

        

          5
          
P is the steer-back power

          
Over and over, the robot asks its gyro: “How far off am I?” Then it does a times problem:

          
steer = P × how far off

          
One wheel gets that much more power. The other gets that much less. So the robot turns back to straight.

          

            
            
          

          
        

        

      


      

        

          6
          
How much P?

          
Four robots, each with the same weak motor and the same bumps. Only P is different.

          
    
            
  • P = 0: it never steers back, so it drifts far away.
    • 
            
  • Too small: it steers back too gently, so it still drifts a bit.
    • 
            
  • Just right: it stays on the path.
    • 
            
  • Too big: it steers back too hard, goes past straight, and wiggles.
    • 
          

        

        

      


      

        

          7
          
The robot's loop

          
Your code does these four steps again and again, 100 times every second! That is how it keeps fixing tiny mistakes before they get big.

          
🐢 Here it is slowed way down so you can see each step.

        

        

      


      

        

          8
          
Your tuning recipe

          

            
1F first
Change F until your robot keeps up with the ghost.

            
2Then P
Turn P up until the robot stays on the path.

            
3Wiggling?
P is too big. Turn it down a little.

          

          
        

      


      

        

          9
          
What about I and D?

          
Robot engineers use two more letters, I and D. The whole family is called PIDF. I and D help with harder jobs, like stopping exactly on a spot. For driving straight, F and P do the job, so I and D are locked for now.

        

      

    

  


  
  

    

      

        

          
The Pybricks program

          
This uses your F and P. Paste it into code.pybricks.com and run it on a SPIKE Prime hub with Pybricks. Left wheel motor in port A, right wheel motor in port B.

        

        
      

      

      
📱 iPads can run this simulator, but they can't send code to the robot. Pybricks connects to the hub over Bluetooth from Chrome or Edge on a Chromebook, Windows, Mac, Linux or Android. iPad and iPhone are not supported (Pybricks: what do you need?). Python coding in Pybricks is free; block coding needs a Pybricks supporter license.

      
A real robot won't use exactly the same numbers, because every robot is a little different. Start with the simulator's F and P, then tune the real robot the same way: F first, then P.
